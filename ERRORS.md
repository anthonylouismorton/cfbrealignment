# Data Audit — `updatedConferenceData.json`

Generated by an automated pass over all 33 conferences and 397 school-membership
entries in `app/data/updatedConferenceData.json`, plus a cross-check of every
`logo` path against `public/images/conferences/` and `public/images/schools/`.
This is a report only — nothing in the data files was changed.

## How this was checked

- Conference-level: `founded`/`disbanded` ordering, `majorConference` range
  sanity, `names[]` era coverage (gaps/overlaps, whether it reaches
  `disbanded`).
- School-level: coordinates in a plausible US range, `stateId` present,
  `years[]` sorted/deduplicated and within the conference's own
  founded–disbanded window, and each `left[]` entry checked against the end of
  its *own* stint (schools that left and later rejoined are common in the
  older conferences, so this compares against the nearest prior active year,
  not just the school's overall last year).
- Logos: every `logo` path (conference and school) checked for existence on
  disk; every file on disk checked for being referenced by something; school
  logos checked for accidental reuse across two different schools.

---

## 1. Systemic issue: several `left` entries describe the wrong departure

This is the highest-value find — the same bug shape shows up on **7 different
schools across 5 conferences**, so it's worth fixing as a pattern rather than
one-off edits. In each case, a school's *most recent real-world conference
move* appears to have been pasted onto an old, already-defunct conference
record for that school, instead of describing that school's actual departure
from *that* conference.

| Conference record | `left` entry says | What the school's own `years[]` actually shows |
|---|---|---|
| Big 8 — University of Oklahoma | left in **2024** for SEC | Active only through 1995 (Big 8 dissolved into Big 12 in 1996) |
| Southwest Conference — University of Oklahoma | left in **1996** for Big 12 | Active only 1915–1918 |
| Southwest Conference — Oklahoma State University | left in **1996** for Big 12 | Active only 1915–1924 |
| Skyline Conference — Colorado State University | left in **2024** for Big 12 | Active only through 1961; CSU has never been a Big 12 member — this looks like it was meant for University of Colorado Boulder instead |
| Skyline Conference — University of Denver | left in **2024** for FBS | Active only through 1961; Denver dropped football entirely in 1961 and has never fielded an FBS/FCS team since |
| American Athletic Conference — University of South Florida | left in **2013** for **AAC** | Self-referential — USF's `years[]` shows it joining AAC in 2013 and still active through 2026, so "left AAC to join AAC" is nonsensical |
| Missouri Valley Conference — Grinnell College | left in **1986**, destination `null` | Active only through 1936 |
| Missouri Valley Conference — Washington University in St. Louis | left in **1986**, destination `null` | Active only through 1942 |

The MVC two look like a different flavor of the same root cause: MVC's own
`disbanded` year (1986) appears to have been stamped onto every remaining
school's `left` entry regardless of when that specific school actually
stopped playing in the conference (both left decades earlier).

**Suggested fix:** for each row above, either remove the erroneous `left`
entry from the old conference's record, or replace it with the correct
year/destination for that school's actual departure from *that* conference.

## 2. Conference-level logic

- **Ivy League**: `majorConference.start` is **1933**, but `founded` is
  **1956** — a major-conference window can't begin 23 years before the
  conference existed. Possibly intentional (Ivy schools were nationally
  prominent well before the 1954 formal agreement), but worth confirming —
  if it's meant to track the earlier informal "Ivy Group" era, that's a
  different thing from `founded` and may deserve its own field or a comment.

- **Minor / likely intentional** — four conferences have their most recent
  `names[]` era ending exactly one year before the conference's `disbanded`
  year: SIAA (era ends 1941, disbanded 1942), Big East (2012 vs. 2013),
  Border (1961 vs. 1962), SWC (1995 vs. 1996). This is consistent across all
  four, which suggests it's a deliberate convention (`years[]` = last season
  actually played, `disbanded` = first year the conference no longer existed)
  rather than four separate mistakes — flagging in case it's not.

## 3. Worth a human double-check (plausible history, not clear-cut)

Cumberland University's SIAA gap (previously flagged here) has been resolved
— confirmed against Cumberland's own athletics site that the program dropped
football after 1906 and didn't resume until 1912, outside the SIAA. Data
updated accordingly.

## 4. Broken reference — RESOLVED

- **Southwestern University (SWC)** — its `left` entry names `"TIAA"` as the
  destination conference. This used to point at nothing; **TIAA has since
  been added as a full conference** (see § 7 below), so the reference now
  resolves correctly.

## 5. Logos

- **`FBS.png` and `FCS.png` exist in `public/images/conferences/` but aren't
  wired up** — both the `FBS` and `FCS` conference entries have `logo: null`
  even though the matching image files are sitting right there unused. Likely
  just a missed `logo` field.
- Every other conference and all 397 school `logo` paths resolve to a real
  file — no missing images, no accidental reuse of one school's logo on
  another, and no corrupt/empty (near-0-byte) image files anywhere in
  `public/images/conferences/` or `public/images/schools/`.

## 6. Not a bug — documented for context

- **University of Iowa is listed active in both Big Ten and Big 8 for 1907
  and 1908 simultaneously.** This looks like a data conflict at first glance,
  but it's already deliberately handled in `app/functions/getConInfo.js` —
  those two years special-case Iowa's rendering (its normal per-conference
  fill is skipped and replaced with a dual-color entry) because Iowa's
  membership was genuinely disputed between the two conferences those
  seasons. No action needed; noting it here so the reason for that special
  case in the code doesn't get lost.

## 7. New: Texas Intercollegiate Athletic Association (TIAA) added

Added as a full conference (1909–1932, `mapColor: #1F78B4`) to resolve the
broken reference in § 4. Built from the TIAA's own Wikipedia page plus
season-by-season football team pages for individual schools. 16 of 21
schools have solid or reasonably-bracketed years with correct `left`/
`rejoined` entries. The rest have real, sourced gaps rather than guessed
dates:

| School | Status |
|---|---|
| Stephen F. Austin State University | `years: []` — no join year documented anywhere found; confirmed a member by 1929 and a 1931 Lone Star Conference founder, but the start is a total unknown |
| Texas A&M University–Commerce (East Texas at the time) | `years: []` — same situation as Stephen F. Austin: confirmed member by 1929, no documented join year |
| Hardin-Simmons University (Simmons University at the time) | `years: []` — confirmed a member by the mid-1920s and part of the 1925 five-school split to the (untracked) Texas Conference, but no join year found |
| Texas A&M University–Kingsville (Texas A&I at the time) | `years: []` — Wikipedia's own pages contradict each other here: the program's conference-history infobox says only 1930, but the 1932 season article lists TIAA as that year's conference too, and other sources claim TIAA titles in both 1931 and 1932. Not resolved, flagged rather than picked arbitrarily |
| Daniel Baker College | `years: [1924, 1925, 1926]` — directly confirmed via three individual season pages (1924, 1925, and the 1926 TIAA championship). No page exists for 1922, 1923, or 1927, so `left` is set to 1927 as a placeholder, but the true join year (pre-1924) and actual departure are still unconfirmed |

Also note: the destinations the 1931 Lone Star Conference founders moved to
still aren't tracked in this dataset (same situation TIAA itself was in
before this fix) — their `left` entries use `"FBS"` as a generic placeholder.
**The four schools that left TIAA for the Texas Conference in 1926
(Southwestern, Austin College, Trinity, Howard Payne) have since been
updated to point at `"TXCON"` instead**, now that it's tracked (see § 8).

## 9. New: Lone Star Conference (LSC) added — still active, largest scope yet

Added as a conference founded 1931, **`disbanded: null`** — unlike TIAA and
Texas Conference, this one is still running today (18 full members plus 2
football-only affiliates as of 2025). This closes out the `"FBS"`
placeholders that were left in TIAA (Sam Houston, North Texas, Texas State,
all departing 1931) and Texas Conference (Texas A&M-Kingsville, departing
1954) — those four now point at `"LSC"` directly.

**Color**: `#C05FB2` (magenta/orchid) — a genuinely new addition to the
palette, not a reuse. LSC's 1931–present span is long enough that *every one*
of the 14 existing map colors gets used by something else at some point
within it, so nothing could be safely reused this time.

**Scope note — only football-playing members included.** The LSC's current
"18 full members" is an all-sports count; only 10 of those 18 actually field
football (the rest — Dallas Baptist, Lubbock Christian, Oklahoma Christian,
St. Edward's, St. Mary's, TAMIU, UT Dallas, UT Tyler, Texas Woman's — have no
football program at all, confirmed via each school's own page). **Cameron**
is a current all-sports LSC member but dropped football after 1992 and never
brought it back — excluded here for the same reason. Two future members
(Texas A&M-Texarkana, Texas Wesleyan, both joining 2026–2028) aren't
included since they're beyond this dataset's 2026 horizon and haven't
actually joined yet.

**30 schools added.** This research pass surfaced far more cross-source
contradiction than TIAA or Texas Conference did — nearly every school that
went through the 1990s Oklahoma/Arkansas expansion wave has a 1–2 year
discrepancy between the LSC's own summary Wikipedia page and that school's
individual football program page. Convention used throughout: when the two
disagree, the school's own team page wins (same reasoning as before — it's
consistently been the more reliable source type across all three of these
conference buildouts). Every case where that rule was applied is listed
below rather than silently absorbed:
